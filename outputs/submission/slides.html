The argument for two arms

One arm cannot lift a plate. It levers it.

A single gripper pinches the rim 94 mm from the plate's centre of mass, so it applies almost pure torque. The plate tips up on its far rim, the centre height rises, and the plate never leaves the table.

That single-arm teacher is kept in the tree at 0/10 and runs in the demo on its own hotkey. It is the evidence for the whole design, not a result to hide.

What "success" means here

A gate the robot has to pass with physics

  • Force-bearing contact from the named gripper pads, read through mj_contactForce, not proximity.
  • Sustained lift with nothing outside the robot bearing the weight, so a plate still resting on the table cannot score.
  • Stable release at resting height after the gripper opens.
  • Levelness, for the two-arm lift: over 15 degrees at any airborne moment and the episode fails.

No welds, no teleports, no raised motor force limits, and the drawer joint is never driven directly. Workcell setup happens only before an episode and is declared in the result.

Failed episodes are preserved and labelled, not deleted.

The cooperative action

Two arms cancel the torque one arm creates

Both arms close on opposing rim segments, 188 mm apart. The two contact wrenches cancel about the plate's centre, so the net action is a lift rather than a pivot.

The plate then travels to a mat inside the two-arm overlap and is released standing.

  • Peak lift 57-60 mm, peak tilt while airborne 12.3-13.8 deg, across the ten held-out seeds
  • Four-pad force-bearing contact 9.1 s, of which 4.6 s airborne and level
  • Released on the mat within 4-25 mm of its centre

Every skill has a run that must score zero

Chaining without a reset

The fork comes out of the drawer the robot just opened

One episode, no reset between skills, no weld, no teleport. The drawer stage must first travel at least 8.5 cm through a verified bilateral handle pull, and then stay open with the whole arm off the whole drawer: an arm leaning on the drawer front fails the gate.

Only then does the fork retrieval run, and it depends on the physical state the first half produced.

Perception and planning

Calibrated vision, and a planner that fails closed

Camera-guided cup place: 50/50. The cup is located from top-camera pixels by calibrated colour segmentation, and the skill runs from that estimate. The policy is given no object poses.

The planner is a deterministic grammar, not a VLM. It previews a skill sequence, derives which arm owns each object from the measured ownership table, and then refuses to execute anything without a measured held-out success rate.

Ask it to pour and it previews pick(right, bottle) -> pour(right, bottle) and then declines to run it. Failing closed is the feature.

Learning and the Intel toolchain

ACT trained locally, exported to OpenVINO

  • LeRobot 0.6.0 ACT, frozen pretrained ResNet18, RGB plus 12 joint positions, 20-action chunks.
  • Normalization from training episodes only; validation and test seeds cannot overlap training.
  • Exported to OpenVINO FP32 and running locally, past numerical parity fixtures.
  • The FP16 candidate failed parity and is rejected. INT8 is not done.

The demo still runs the proven teacher. A learned policy gets promoted when its held-out physical rollouts justify it, not when its offline loss looks good.

No Intel result
Everything here ran on Apple silicon. Mac CPU inference numbers are diagnostics and are not challenge results. The qualifying Core Ultra Series 2/3 run has not happened.
Do not quote an Intel latency or throughput figure from this project. There isn't one.
What is not done

The honest half of the scoreboard

  • Pouring. The bottle now has a graspable 32 mm neck, and a kinematic probe found 17 graspable bottle poses with viable bottle/mug pairs up to 216 mm apart. There is still no pour skill, and MuJoCo has no liquid in this scene.
  • The spoon handoff. Gates are wired and demand a measured receiver lift, but no passing run exists.
  • Chaining beyond drawer to fork. The full dinner-table sequence is not implemented.
  • INT8 quantization and the Intel Core Ultra run.
